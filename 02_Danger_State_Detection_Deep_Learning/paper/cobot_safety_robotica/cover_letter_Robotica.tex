We are pleased to submit our original research manuscript entitled \textbf{``Danger-State Detection in Human-Robot Collaboration Using Deep Learning''} by Ameur Gargouri, Mohamed Karray, and Mohamed Ksantini for consideration for publication as a regular research article in \textit{Robotica}.

\vspace{0.3em}
{\large\textbf{Context and Motivation}}\par\vspace{2pt}
As industrial automation transitions toward Industry 5.0, physical Human-Robot Collaboration (pHRC) has become essential for flexible and intelligent manufacturing. Safe co-existence and direct physical interaction between human workers and collaborative robots (cobots) demand continuous, real-time assessment of workspace hazards in compliance with standards such as ISO/TS 15066. However, current safety mechanisms face a severe \textbf{false-alarm trade-off}: overly sensitive detectors frequently command unwarranted emergency stops that disrupt production cadence and compromise worker trust, whereas insufficiently sensitive systems risk severe operator injury from high-impact collisions, clamping, or crushing events.

Conventional collision avoidance systems typically rely on costly multi-axis force/torque transducers, external multi-camera vision rigs that are prone to line-of-sight occlusions and lighting variations, or disturbance observers that require mathematically exacting nominal dynamic models. Utilizing wearable inertial measurement units (IMUs) provides an attractive, cost-effective, and occlusion-free alternative. Nevertheless, current literature lacks a systematic empirical comparison of modern deep learning sequence architectures on wearable sensor streams, as well as deterministic mechanisms designed specifically to curtail false alarms.

\vspace{0.3em}
{\large\textbf{Key Contributions of the Manuscript}}\par\vspace{2pt}
This work bridges this gap by proposing a complete, end-to-end deep learning framework tailored for robust, high-accuracy, and low-false-alarm danger-state detection:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1.5pt,topsep=1.5pt,partopsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{GPU-Accelerated Kinematic Expansion:} We develop an on-the-fly kinematic derivative pipeline running entirely on GPU, deriving joint velocities and accelerations from raw 65-channel IMU position streams to generate 195 feature channels without additional hardware sensors or offline storage overhead.
    \item \textbf{Systematic Benchmark of Modern 1D Architectures:} We perform a rigorous comparative evaluation of five distinct 1D architectures—ConvNeXt~1D, Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN), MLP-Mixer~1D, Transformer~1D, and InceptionTime—with and without Test-Time Augmentation (TTA). ConvNeXt~1D and TCN achieve state-of-the-art macro-F1 scores of 98.68\% and 98.62\%, respectively, with danger precision exceeding 98\% and recall surpassing 97\%.
    \item \textbf{Dedicated False-Alarm Reduction Post-Processing:} We design a tailored temporal debouncing stage integrating a 3-tap median filter with decision threshold optimization. This mechanism reduces false alarms by \textbf{87.5\%} while maintaining danger detection recall above 97.1\%, establishing a viable operating point for industrial deployment.
    \item \textbf{Rigorous Benchmark Validation:} The framework is validated via 5-fold stratified cross-validation over more than 69,000 temporal windows gathered from 60 human subjects on the DASIG benchmark, verifying statistical robustness across diverse operating profiles.
\end{enumerate}
